**10 Rules of Good Swiss Grid Design**  
*(in the spirit of Dieter Rams’ 10 Principles of Good Design)*

1. **Good Swiss Grid Design is systematic.**  
   It begins with a clear, logical grid and follows it rigorously. Arbitrary placement is chaos.

2. **Good Swiss Grid Design serves the content.**  
   The grid is not decoration — it is infrastructure. It organizes information so the message becomes clear and accessible.

3. **Good Swiss Grid Design creates order without boredom.**  
   It uses asymmetry, rhythm, and proportional relationships to bring life to strict geometry.

4. **Good Swiss Grid Design is precise.**  
   Every millimeter, every baseline, every margin matters. Sloppiness destroys credibility.

5. **Good Swiss Grid Design achieves clarity through reduction.**  
   It removes the unnecessary so the essential can speak with maximum power.

6. **Good Swiss Grid Design respects typography.**  
   Text is not filler. It is given generous space, proper hierarchy, and breathing room on the baseline grid.

7. **Good Swiss Grid Design creates visual tension and harmony.**  
   It balances strong contrasts (scale, weight, color) with quiet, harmonious structure.

8. **Good Swiss Grid Design is timeless rather than fashionable.**  
   It avoids trends and cheap effects. A well-designed grid still looks contemporary in twenty years.

9. **Good Swiss Grid Design is honest.**  
   It does not hide behind ornament or unnecessary decoration. Form follows function, and structure is visible when it serves the message.

10. **Good Swiss Grid Design is thorough to the last detail.**  
    Even the smallest element — a caption, a folio number, a hairline rule — is considered with the same care as the headline.
